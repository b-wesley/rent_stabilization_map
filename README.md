# [Map of Change in Rent-Stabilized Units](https://b-wesley.github.io/rent_stabilization_map/)

When New York passed the Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (HSTPA), it became illegal for landlords to remove apartments from rent stabilization with a few very specific exceptions. This map uses DOF property tax bill data to locate buildings where the number of Rent-Stabilized Units has changed between 2019 and 2024. 

Using data from the Furman Center and the NYC Department of Finance, this map illustrates the change in rent-stabilized units at both the building and community district level. The rent-stabilized units can be filtered by whether or not they are programmatic rent stabilized units (units that are subject to rent stabilization because they receive public funding or tax incentives. Certain programs that fall under this programmatic umbrella, such as 421-a and J-51, eventually expire, and can remove their units from rent stabilization once this happens. However, not all programmatic units are under programs that allow owners to de-stabilize units, so a building's presence in this category does not necessarily indicate that it has been illegally de-stabilized. However, there is no legal mechanism for de-stabilization of non-programmatic units post-HSTPA.

Click on the map to see high-level stats about a Community District's rent-stabilized housing stock or hover over a building to see how many rent-stabilized units it has lost or gained since 2019.
